## Version A — full (~1,500 chars / ~265 words)

One of the best uses of AI for analysts is something almost no one talks about: REPURPOSING.

Most analytical work isn't done when the analysis is done. It's done when:

→ The CFO has the tight version
→ The VP of Engineering has the version focused on her line
→ The board has the strategic frame
→ All of it lives in the right format — email, deck, memo, Slack

That part isn't analysis. It's repackaging. And it eats hours every week.

It's also exactly what AI is best at — if you set it up right.

Two axes do most of the work.

AUDIENCE SHIFT. Same content, different reader.
A CFO wants three bullets and two specific asks. A department head wants context for their own line. A board wants the strategic picture and no line-item detail. The numbers are constant. The framing isn't.

FORMAT SHIFT. Same content, different deliverable.
The same findings become an email, a deck, a Slack post, a one-pager. The underlying analysis is the same. The wrapper is what's changing.

Most of what eats your afternoons is some combination of those two.

The key: stop re-explaining the rules every time you ask. Write them down once — into a saved skill, a system prompt, a template — and reach for them whenever you need a CFO email or a deck outline.

You'll get the rest of your week back.

## A note on tone

Both versions avoid emoji and stay close to your blog voice. The full version uses arrow bullets for scannability — those mirror the one-pager and read well on LinkedIn without leaning into emoji territory.
